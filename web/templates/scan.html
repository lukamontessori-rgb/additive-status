Label checker

Paste or type the ingredients list from a food label. The additives it recognises are picked out and shown with their status in the five places.

{{ icon('lock', 15) }} The text stays on your device. It is checked in your browser and never sent anywhere.

    It finds E-numbers (E 102, E-102, e102), INS numbers in brackets as used on Australian and New Zealand labels (“colour (102)”), and additive names such as “citric acid”. Names the lists do not use, and additives that are not on any of the five lists, are not found.

    Good to know

    • A label can be right even when a status says "not allowed". A product made for one market follows that market's rules. Imported food can also be covered by rules not shown here.
    • "Allowed" comes with conditions. Most additives are permitted only in certain foods and up to a maximum level. Open an additive to see the conditions.
    • Flavourings, enzymes and processing aids often do not appear as additives on a label and are mostly not covered.
