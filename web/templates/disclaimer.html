Not legal, medical or nutritional advice

Nothing on this site is legal, regulatory, medical, dietary, nutritional or professional advice, and using it does not create a client, patient or advisory relationship with anyone. If a decision matters, read the official source linked on each page and ask the competent authority or a qualified professional.

Not a safety verdict

Whether a substance is on an official list, or off it, is a legal fact about one place at one time. It is not a statement that the substance is safe or unsafe. The site does not rate, score or rank additives for health. If you have an allergy, a medical condition or a dietary need, talk to a doctor or dietitian and read product labels.

Accuracy, completeness and timing

  • Automated summaries. Statuses are produced automatically from official files and then simplified into a few plain categories. Reading and matching can go wrong, even though the process is tested and every match shows how it was made.
  • Not up to the minute. The data is checked about once a week. Laws change between checks, and an official source can lag behind the law. Each page shows when its data was last checked.
  • Not the official text. Only the official publications are authoritative. For EU law, only the acts in the Official Journal of the European Union are authentic. The linked source always wins over this site.
  • If a source fails. A download that fails or looks wrong never replaces the last good data. The site then shows older data and says so on the about page.
  • No guarantee. No promise is made that the site is complete, error-free or always available.

What "allowed", "not allowed" and "not listed" mean here

"Allowed" means the substance is authorised or in a phase-out period on that place's official list. "Not allowed" means the list says it is not authorised, prohibited or removed. "Not listed" means the substance was not found on the list we read, which is not the same as "not allowed": some places permit substances by other routes (for example, the United States has substances that are generally recognised as safe without being on the inventory). The full definitions are on the about page. A word such as "banned" in other media may mean something different.

What is not covered

  • Only five places are covered: the European Union, Great Britain, the United States (federal level), Canada, and Australia/New Zealand. Other countries, and US state and local laws, are not covered.
  • The site shows lists of additives. It does not cover contaminants, pesticide residues, novel foods, food contact materials, labelling rules or acceptable daily intake values. Flavourings are not covered systematically, and only parts of the conditions of use are shown.
  • It does not say what is in any particular product or brand.
